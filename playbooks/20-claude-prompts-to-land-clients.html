20 Claude prompts to land local clients

Copy, paste, fill in the [brackets]. They work for any service you sell to local businesses: websites, SEO, Google profiles, reviews, social, ads or automations. Run them in order the first time; after that, use the one you need.

Before you start: turn on web search in Claude, so it checks real listings and sites instead of guessing. With Claude in Chrome it can also read Google Maps and open each website the way a customer sees it. If Claude can't verify something, it should say so: every prompt below asks it to.

1. Pick who you help (3 prompts)

1. Score my niches

I sell [your service] to local businesses in [town] and nearby [town, town]. For each of these trades: [trade 1], [trade 2], [trade 3], search Google Maps for "[trade] in [town]" and tell me, from what you can actually see: how many listings there are, how many have no website or a broken one, how many have 20+ reviews at 4.5★ or better, and how many have fewer than 10 reviews. Then score each trade 1–5 on job value, gaps, busy, reachable. Show the counts, say which numbers you couldn't check, and recommend one trade and one area.

2. Write my one-line offer

I help [trade] in [area]. I sell [service]. Write 5 versions of a one-line offer that sells the outcome the owner wants (more calls, booked jobs, fewer no-shows), not the service. Under 25 words each, no hype, no promised results. Then tell me which one a busy owner would understand fastest and why.

3. Build my three packages

Using my offer "[offer]", design three packages: Starter, a recommended middle one, and Grow. For each: what's included in plain words, one-time vs monthly, and the time it takes me. Leave the prices as [$] for me to fill in, then tell me the typical market range for each from sources you can cite, with the source and year.

2. Find the businesses (6 prompts)

4. No website, great reviews

Search Google Maps for "[trade] in [town]". List every business with 20+ reviews at 4.3★ or better that has no website button. For each: name, rating, review count, phone, the Maps link, and whether a web search finds any site for them. Only list ones you checked. Put it in a table, best first.

5. Broken or outdated websites

Here are [trade] websites in [town]: [paste URLs, or "find the first 30 on Google Maps"]. Open each one and flag: doesn't load, domain expired or parked, "Not secure" warning, not mobile-friendly, copyright year 2021 or older, no phone number above the fold, no way to book or request a quote. One line of proof per flag. Skip the ones that are fine.

6. Brand-new businesses

Open [your city's business license open-data link]. Find licenses issued in the last 14 days for [categories, e.g. food, retail, personal services, contractors]. For the 15 that look most like walk-in or local-service businesses, check whether each has a Google listing and a website. Table: name, category, issue date, on Google? website? Don't include anyone's home address or personal details.

7. Reviews that complain about the website or booking

For these businesses: [names or Maps links], search their Google reviews for "website", "book online", "couldn't find", "hours", "call back", "no answer". Quote the review line (under 20 words) and the date for each hit. Those are the problems the owner already knows about.

8. People asking for help online

Search Reddit, local Facebook groups that are public, Nextdoor posts that are public and local forums for people in [town] asking for a [your service] recommendation or complaining about [problem you fix] in the last 90 days. Give me the link, the date and one line on what they asked. Then write a helpful reply for each that answers the question and doesn't pitch.

9. Partners who already get asked

List 20 local businesses in [town] whose customers often need [your service]: accountants, bookkeepers, insurance brokers, sign and print shops, photographers, business coaches, coworking spaces, the chamber of commerce. Name, what they do, phone or contact page. Then write a 60-word intro message offering them something useful for their clients (not a referral fee pitch).

3. Research one lead before you reach out (3 prompts)

10. What their customers see today

Business: [name, Maps link]. Google them the way a customer would: "[trade] near [town]", their name, and their name + "reviews". Tell me what shows up first, what's missing or wrong (hours, photos, services, no site, competitor ads), how their reviews compare with the top 3 in the map results, and the single most obvious fix. Plain words, one page, only things you saw.

11. Do AI answers recommend them?

Answer as you normally would: "Who's the best [trade] in [town]?" and "[trade] near [neighbourhood] open now". Then tell me: is [business] mentioned? Which businesses are, and what do their websites and listings have that [business]'s doesn't (service pages, FAQs, reviews, consistent name/address/phone)?

12. The gap vs their top competitor

Compare [business] with [competitor] on: review count and recency, photos, posts, website pages per service and per town, page speed on mobile, online booking, how fast they reply. Table, then the 3 gaps that would matter most to a customer.

4. Reach out (4 prompts)

13. My first message

Using this lead brief: [paste the result of prompt 10], write my first message for [walk-in / phone / DM / email]. First line about them and true. Under 80 words. Ask for one small thing (can I show you / send you the link). No fake urgency, no promised results. If email: include [my name, business, phone, mailing address] and an opt-out line.

14. Two follow-ups, then stop

Write two follow-ups for that message: one for day 3–4 (on a different channel if I have one), one for day 10–14 that closes the loop politely. Each shorter than the one before.

15. Practise the call

Role-play the owner of [business], a busy [trade] owner who didn't ask for this call. Be realistic: interrupt, say "how did you get my number", "I have a guy", "how much". I'll pitch [service]. After 5 exchanges, stop and tell me what I said that worked and what to cut.

16. Answer their reply

The owner replied: "[paste]". Write two short, honest answers: one that moves to a 15-minute call, one that respects a no. Don't argue, don't drop the price.

5. Close (2 prompts)

17. Prep the sales call

I have a call with [business] on [day]. From what you can find, build my one-page prep sheet: what they do, their reviews and rating, what customers see today, the 5 questions I'll ask, and the money math in their numbers (one job worth [$X] × [Y]% of callers booking → how many extra jobs a month cover a [$Z] fee). List the 3 objections I'll most likely hear and an honest answer to each.

18. Write the proposal

Call notes: [paste their answers in their words]. Write a one-page proposal: their goal in their words, what I'll do, the package I recommend and why, the price [$], timeline, what I need from them, payment terms (50% to start, 50% at launch; monthly billed in advance). No promised rankings or numbers of calls.

6. Keep them (2 prompts)

19. The monthly report

Here are this month's and last month's numbers for [client]: [paste: profile calls, website clicks, direction requests, form leads, new reviews, pages published]. Write a one-page report: one honest headline, a table with the change for each number, what I did, the 3 things I'll do next month, and one suggestion. If a number went down, say so and give the likely reason. Then a 3-line email to send it with.

20. Ask for the referral (and the case study)

[Client] just [launched / had a good month: what happened]. Write: a short message asking who else they know who'd want this, a forwardable text they can send a friend about me, and 5 questions for a case study they can answer in 5 minutes.
