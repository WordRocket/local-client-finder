# The one-page monthly client report

In vcita's 2025 survey of 500 US small businesses, the #1 reason owners dropped their marketing agency was no return
on investment (60%). Usually that means they couldn't see what they were paying for. This page makes the work visible in 10
minutes a month. Send it in the first week of every month, every month, even a slow one.

---

## The template (copy it into a doc, keep it to one page)

**[Client business] · [Month Year] report**

**Headline:** [one honest sentence: the biggest change this month, good or bad]

| Number | This month | Last month | Change | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calls from the Google profile | | | | Google Business Profile › Performance › Calls |
| Website clicks from the profile | | | | Google Business Profile › Performance › Website clicks |
| Direction requests | | | | Google Business Profile › Performance › Directions |
| Clicks from Google Search to the site | | | | Google Search Console › Performance |
| Form leads / bookings | | | | the form inbox, booking tool, or GA4 key events |
| New reviews (and new average) | | | | the Google profile |
| Pages published or updated | | | | your work log |

**What I did this month**
- [3–6 bullets, in plain words: "Added a page for deck repair in Broken Arrow", "Answered 4 reviews"]

**Next month**
1. [the most useful thing]
2. [second]
3. [third]

**One suggestion:** [the one thing THEY could do that would help most, e.g. "ask the 10 customers from this month for a
review", "add photos of the new truck"]

---

## How to fill it in without getting it wrong
- **Pick 4–6 numbers and keep the same ones every month,** so the trend means something. Don't swap in a better-looking
  number when one dips.
- **Compare the same length of time.** Month vs last month, and once you have a year of data, vs the same month last
  year (seasonal trades swing a lot).
- **If a number went down, say so first** and give the likely reason: season, a holiday, a competitor's new ads, a
  Google change. Hiding it is how trust ends.
- **Never claim credit you can't show.** "Calls went up 18%" is a fact; "my work brought you 18% more calls" usually
  isn't provable. Say what you did and what changed.
- **Leads beat traffic.** Owners care about calls, bookings and forms. Put those first, impressions last or not at all.
- **Note anything unusual:** a week the profile was suspended, a site outage, a price change, a big job that skews the
  month.

## The 3-line email to send it with

> Subject: [Business] · [Month] report
>
> Hi [Name], your [Month] report is attached. The short version: [headline]. Next month I'm focusing on [#1 from the
> list]. Anything you'd like me to look at?

## Example (made-up numbers, to show the shape)

**Example Pressure Washing · October report**

**Headline:** Profile calls are up for the third month running, and the new driveway-cleaning page is already
getting clicks.

| Number | October | September | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile calls | 41 | 35 | +6 |
| Website clicks | 58 | 52 | +6 |
| Directions | 9 | 12 | −3 (fewer walk-ins after the season) |
| Search clicks to the site | 214 | 187 | +27 |
| Quote requests | 11 | 8 | +3 |
| New reviews | 6 (4.9★) | 3 (4.9★) | +3 |

What I did: driveway-cleaning page, Owasso and Jenks town pages, 4 profile posts, replied to every review.
Next month: a gutter-cleaning page, winter hours on the profile, the review-request text after every job.
One suggestion: send me photos from 3 jobs a month; listings with fresh photos look more active.
