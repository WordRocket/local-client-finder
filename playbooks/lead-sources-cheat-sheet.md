# 8 places your next client is hiding

A cheat sheet for finding local businesses that need a website. Most people only try one (Google Maps). Use three.
Every source here is public. Never use an owner's personal phone, email or home address.

---

## 1. New business licenses (be first in the door)
Most cities publish every new business license as open data, often updated daily.
- **Find it:** search `"[your city]" open data "business license"`, open the dataset, sort by issue date.
- **Good lead:** a storefront or service business that opened in the last 30 days. Skip home-based licenses, renewals and
  chains.
- **First move:** "Congrats on opening. I noticed you're not on Google yet / don't have a site, so I made you a preview."
- **Scale:** Chicago issued 286 new business licenses from October 15 to 28, 2026. In a spot check of 12 new shops and
  restaurants (Oct 2026), 8 had no website or weren't on Google Maps. Where to find your city's list: the free "Where 30
  cities publish brand-new businesses" PDF.
- *Automated by the free* `find-new-businesses` *skill.*

## 2. Great reviews, no website (Google Maps)
- **Find it:** Google Maps › `[trade] in [town]` › look for listings with no Website button.
- **Good lead:** 20+ reviews at 4.5★ or better. Busy and trusted, missing the obvious. Check with a quick web search
  that they really have no site (some link Facebook instead).
- **Scale:** in our test, 32 of 115 Chicago handymen had no website (Oct 2026).
- *Automated by the free* `find-clients` *skill.*

## 3. Broken or outdated websites
They already paid for a website once, so they get it.
- **Look for:** domains that don't load, "Not secure" warnings, error pages, "this domain is for sale", © 2019 in the
  footer, no mobile layout.
- **First move:** lead with the screenshot of what their customers see today. Confirm in a real browser first: never
  pitch a false alarm.
- **Scale:** 10 of 70 Chicago handyman websites we checked were broken (Oct 2026).
- *Automated by the free* `find-broken-sites` *skill.*

## 4. Reviews that complain about the website or booking
- **Find it:** in their Google or Yelp reviews, search for "website", "book online", "couldn't find", "hours were wrong",
  "menu".
- **Good lead:** a recent review where a customer struggled online. The owner already knows it's a problem.
- **First move:** quote the problem (not the reviewer's name), then show the fix.

## 5. People asking for help online
- **Find it:** `site:reddit.com "[city]" "web designer"` (or "website"), your local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, local
  "gigs" listings.
- **Good lead:** a post from the last few months by a business you can identify.
- **First move:** answer the question helpfully in public first. Offer help only if it fits. No copy-paste pitches.

## 6. Partners who serve the same owners
Other people get asked "do you know a website person?" every week.
- **Who:** accountants and bookkeepers, insurance brokers, business bankers, sign shops, printers, photographers,
  commercial cleaners, local IT people.
- **First move:** "I build websites for [trade] in [area]. When your clients ask you about a website, can I be the
  person you send them to? Happy to send people your way too."
- **Make it easy:** a one-page referral card, and a thank-you (or a referral fee, in writing) for every client.

## 7. Rooms full of owners
- **Where:** chamber of commerce mixers, networking chapters (BNI and similar), trade association meetings, local
  business expos, a talk at the library ("Get found on Google").
- **First move:** ask about their business, not yours. Show a preview site on your phone only when they ask what you do.
- **Goal:** one real conversation and one follow-up per event.

## 8. Your own network (the warm 50)
Word of mouth is still the #1 way local owners get new customers (35% in BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 owners),
and in a GoDaddy survey of 204 owners who'd hired a web pro (2021), 73% found them through recommendations.
- **Do it:** list 50 people you know (family, friends, old coworkers, your barber, your mechanic).
- **Ask:** "Quick one: who do you know who runs a business with no website, or an old one? I'm helping local businesses
  get found on Google." That's it. Don't pitch them, ask for the introduction.

## The 20-in-20 routine
20 minutes a day, 20 leads in a simple sheet (name, trade, area, source, why they need it, phone, next step):
- 10 from Google Maps (source 2)
- 5 broken sites (source 3)
- 5 new licenses (source 1)
Then contact the best 5 the same day. Walk in or call before you email.
