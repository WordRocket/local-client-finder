# Outreach scripts that get replies

Copy, paste, make them yours. Every script opens with something true about THEM, is short, and asks for one small thing.

---

## The order that works for local businesses
Freelancers consistently report the same ranking for local owners: **walk in > call > DM > email.** Email is the easiest
to send and the easiest to ignore, so use it after a call or walk-in, or when a business only publishes an email.

Three rules for every channel:
1. **The first line is about them:** their reviews, their broken page, their opening, a question a customer asked.
2. **Short.** Studies of millions of cold emails (Hunter, Belkins) keep finding the same thing: short messages and one or
   two follow-ups get the most replies; long pitches get ignored.
3. **One message per business, written for that business.** No blasts.

---

## Walk-in (20 seconds, storefronts, salons, restaurants)
Go at a quiet time (mid-morning or mid-afternoon). Ask for the owner by name if you know it.
> "Hi, is [owner] around? I'm [name], I build websites for local [trade]. I saw you have [87] great reviews on Google but
> no website, so I made you a quick preview. Can I show you? Takes 30 seconds."

Show the preview on your phone. Leave your one-pager. If it's a bad time: "No problem, when's better?"

## Phone call (30 seconds)
> "Hi, is this [owner]? I'm [name], I'm local, I build websites for [trade]. Quick reason for the call: you've got [4.9
> stars from 112 reviews], but when people Google you they land on [Yelp / nothing / a page that won't load]. I built a
> preview of what your site could look like. Can I text or email you the link?"
Got the email? Send the preview within 10 minutes.

## Voicemail (20 seconds)
> "Hi [owner], [name] here, local web designer. You've got great reviews, and I built a free preview of a website for
> [business]. I'll email it over. If you want it, call me back at [number]. Again, [number]."

## DM (businesses that only have Facebook or Instagram)
> "Hi [name], love your work on [the post/job]. I noticed your page is your main 'website', so I built you a proper one
> from your Google listing, free to look at: [link]. Want me to put it live on your own domain?"

## Cold email (about 50–80 words, only to an email the business publishes)
**Subject:** a website for [Business Name]
> Hi [Name],
>
> [Specific, true line: "Your 112 reviews say you're the best deck builder in Broken Arrow, but Googling you lands on
> Yelp."] I built a preview of what your site could look like: [link]
>
> Want me to put it live on your own domain this week?
>
> [Your name] · [business] · [phone] · [business mailing address]
> Reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.

## The follow-ups (two, shorter each time, then stop)
- **Day 3–4, a different channel if you can:** "Hi [Name], just checking you saw the preview I made for [Business]: [link].
  Happy to change anything."
- **Day 10–14:** "Last note from me: the preview's still here if you ever want it: [link]. All the best with the season."
Then stop. No reply after the third touch means not now.

## Warm intro (when a friend gives you a name)
Ask your friend to text first: "Hey [owner], my friend [your name] builds websites for local businesses, worth a chat."
Then you: "Hi [owner], [friend] said I should reach out. I had a look at [business] online and made you a quick preview..."

## When they answer
- **"How much?"** "Most owners go with [middle package] at [price]. Before we talk numbers, can I show you what you'd get?"
- **"Who is this? How did you get my number?"** "Fair question: it's on your Google listing. I'm [name], local. I'm not
  asking for money today, I just built you a preview."
- **"I have a guy."** "Great, keep the preview as ideas for him. If it ever doesn't work out, I'm here."
- **"Not interested."** "No problem at all, thanks for your time." And stop. Don't contact them again.

---

## The rules (plain words, not legal advice)
- **US email (CAN-SPAM):** no misleading subject lines, include your mailing address and an easy opt-out, honor opt-outs
  within 10 business days.
- **US calls and texts (TCPA):** call by hand; never cold text and never use auto-dialers (many owners list a cell phone).
- **Canada (CASL):** email only addresses a business publishes, about something relevant to that business, with your
  contact details and an unsubscribe.
- **UK/EU:** company addresses are OK with an opt-out in the UK; sole traders and many EU countries need consent first.
- Everywhere: the moment anyone says stop, stop.
