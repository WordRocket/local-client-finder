Where 30 cities publish brand-new businesses

A business that opened last week needs a website, a Google profile, reviews and a way to book, and nobody's pitching it yet. Most big cities publish every new license or business registration as open data. Here's where to find them in 30 cities across the US and Canada, each one opened and checked on November 6, 2026.

How to use any of these in 3 steps

  1. Open the link and filter the date field to the last 14–30 days. Always add "date is on or before today": several files have typo dates in the future. Sort newest first.
  2. Keep only the new ones. Use the "Keep only" column below (for example Chicago's APPLICATION TYPE = ISSUE). "Pending" or "in progress" means they haven't opened yet: the best moment to show up with a preview.
  3. Pick the categories that fit what you sell (restaurants, salons, contractors, shops), check each one on Google Maps (listed? website? reviews?) and put the best 5 in your lead tracker.

On most portals (Chicago, NYC, LA, SF, Seattle, Texas, Calgary, Edmonton…) the filter is under Actions › Query data or Filter, then Export › CSV. On map-style portals (DC, Denver, Sacramento, Miami-Dade, Fort Worth): View data table, filter, then Download › CSV.

Privacy, always: many files also list owner names and home-based businesses. Skip home-based and sole-owner rows, pitch the business at its business address or through its public phone or email, and never repost anyone's personal details. Before emailing, follow CAN-SPAM (US) or CASL (Canada).

Your city isn't here?

  • State and provincial registries (new LLCs and corporations; many are holding companies or home addresses, so check for a storefront or a Google listing first): Colorado (daily) · Connecticut (daily) · Florida Sunbiz daily filings · Oregon (monthly) · Texas permits (any Texas city) · Québec REQ (bulk file) · Ontario Business Registry (search only).
  • A nearby city often publishes: for the Phoenix area, Scottsdale; in Orange County, Anaheim's new licences.
  • Other "just opened" signals: Google Maps listings with only a few, very recent reviews, Yelp's "Hot and New", local news "grand opening" posts, and "coming soon" signs on storefronts.
  • Searched and found nothing public (Nov 2026), so don't waste an evening: Phoenix, San Jose, Oakland, Minneapolis, St. Paul, Detroit, Columbus, Baltimore, Charlotte, Raleigh, Indianapolis, Jacksonville, Tampa, Ottawa. Kansas City and Atlanta publish old snapshots only.
